Conditions treated

What Dr. Awerbuch treats

Depression

Depression is more than sadness. Loss of interest, low energy, poor sleep, changes in appetite, trouble concentrating, and feelings of hopelessness can make ordinary days feel impossible. A careful evaluation looks for contributing factors — medical conditions, medications, substance use, life stressors — before recommending treatment.

  • Major depressive disorder
  • Persistent depressive disorder
  • Depression with anxiety

Treatment-resistant depression

Many people try two or more antidepressants without enough relief. That isn’t a personal failure — and it doesn’t mean nothing will help. Dr. Awerbuch takes a close look at what has been tried (medications, doses, duration, side effects) and whether anything may have been missed, then considers next steps such as medication optimization, augmentation strategies, and TMS.

  • Medication review
  • Augmentation strategies
  • TMS & neuromodulation

Anxiety disorders

Constant worry, racing thoughts, physical tension, panic attacks, or avoiding situations that feel overwhelming can take a toll on work, relationships, and health. Treatment may combine medication with referral to evidence-based therapy, with the goal of relief you can feel and sustain.

  • Generalized anxiety disorder
  • Panic disorder
  • Social anxiety disorder

Obsessive-compulsive disorder

OCD involves unwanted, intrusive thoughts and the urge to perform rituals or mental acts to relieve the distress they cause. It is highly treatable. Care often pairs medication management with specialized therapy such as exposure and response prevention (ERP).

  • Intrusive thoughts
  • Compulsions & rituals
  • OCD with depression or anxiety

Bipolar & other mood disorders

Mood disorders can bring periods of depression, elevated or irritable mood, and changes in sleep and energy. Thoughtful, long-term management focuses on stability, preventing relapse, careful monitoring of medications, and protecting the parts of life that matter most to you.

  • Bipolar I & II disorder
  • Mood instability
  • Medication monitoring

Addiction & substance use

Substance use and mental health are deeply connected, and treating one without the other rarely works. Dr. Awerbuch has a particular focus in addiction medicine and offers nonjudgmental, medically informed care for substance use and the co-occurring conditions that often accompany it — including medications for substance use disorders when appropriate.

  • Alcohol use
  • Opioid use
  • Co-occurring disorders

Serious mental illness

Serious mental illness deserves care that is both expert and respectful. Drawing on extensive inpatient and residency experience, Dr. Awerbuch provides diagnosis, medication management, and ongoing support for people living with psychotic disorders and other serious psychiatric conditions, coordinating closely with the rest of the care team.

  • Schizophrenia
  • Schizoaffective disorder
  • Other psychotic disorders

Services

How care is delivered

Every patient’s plan is different, but most care draws on a combination of these services.

Psychiatric evaluation

An in-depth first visit covering your symptoms, history, past treatments, medical health, and goals, ending with a clear diagnosis and recommendations.

Medication management

Careful prescribing and follow-up that weighs benefits, side effects, and your preferences — adjusting the plan as you progress.

TMS & neuromodulation

Advanced, non-invasive treatment options for depression that hasn’t responded to medication, offered at TheraMind Center of Santa Barbara.

Addiction medicine

Integrated treatment for substance use and co-occurring mental health conditions, without judgment.

Consultation & second opinions

A fresh look at a diagnosis or a treatment plan that isn’t working, with practical recommendations you and your clinicians can use.

Care coordination

With your permission, Dr. Awerbuch works alongside your therapist, primary care physician, and other specialists so your care is connected.

Spotlight

Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation (TMS)

TMS uses a magnetic coil placed gently against the scalp to deliver focused pulses to the prefrontal cortex — a region of the brain involved in mood regulation that can be underactive in depression.

First cleared by the FDA for major depressive disorder in 2008, TMS has since been cleared for additional uses. It is an outpatient treatment: there is no anesthesia, you stay awake throughout, and you can return to your normal activities — including driving — right after each session.

Who may benefit

TMS is most often considered for adults with major depression who haven’t had enough relief from antidepressant medications, or who couldn’t tolerate their side effects. Dr. Awerbuch can also discuss other neuromodulation approaches and whether they may be appropriate for you.

Side effects

The most common side effects are mild scalp discomfort or headache, which usually ease over the first weeks of treatment. Serious side effects, such as seizures, are rare.

What to expect

  1. Step 1

    Consultation

    A psychiatric evaluation to confirm whether TMS is safe and appropriate for you.

  2. Step 2

    Mapping session

    Your first session identifies the precise treatment location and the right strength of stimulation for you.

  3. Step 3

    Treatment course

    Sessions typically take place five days a week over several weeks. You can read, listen to music, or talk during treatment.

  4. Step 4

    Ongoing follow-up

    Progress is tracked throughout, and your overall plan is adjusted as your mood improves.

TMS isn’t right for everyone — for example, people with certain metal implants in or near the head. Many insurance plans cover TMS for depression when specific criteria are met; call the office to discuss your coverage.
