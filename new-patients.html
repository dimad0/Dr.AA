New patients

What to expect when you reach out

Starting psychiatric care can feel like a big step. Here is how the process works, what to bring, and answers to common questions.

Getting started

Four simple steps

  1. 01

    Reach out

    Call (805) 845-3046, email office@aawerbuchmd.com, or send a request online. Family members and referring clinicians are welcome to reach out as well.

  2. 02

    A brief conversation

    The office gathers basic information, answers questions about fit and insurance, and schedules your first visit.

  3. 03

    Your evaluation

    Your first appointment is an extended, in-depth psychiatric evaluation, so there is time to understand your full story.

  4. 04

    Your plan

    You leave with a personalized plan. Follow-up visits track your progress and adjust treatment as needed.

Your first visit

What to bring

Gathering a few things ahead of time helps make the most of your first appointment. Don’t worry if you can’t find everything — bring what you can.

  • Photo ID and insurance card
  • A list of current medications and supplements, with doses
  • Past psychiatric medications — what helped, what didn’t, and any side effects
  • Records from previous psychiatrists, therapists, or hospital stays, if available
  • Contact information for your therapist and primary care physician
  • Any questions you’d like to ask

Insurance & payment

Dr. Awerbuch works with a number of insurance plans. Because coverage varies, please call the office before your first visit to confirm your benefits, any referral requirements, and expected costs.

Good Faith Estimate. If you don’t have insurance or choose not to use it, you have the right to receive a Good Faith Estimate of expected charges under the federal No Surprises Act. Ask the office for one when you schedule, or visit cms.gov/nosurprises.

FAQ

Frequently asked questions

What’s the difference between a psychiatrist and a therapist?

A psychiatrist is a medical doctor (MD) who specializes in mental health. Psychiatrists diagnose conditions, consider medical factors that can affect mood and thinking, prescribe and manage medication, and can offer treatments such as TMS. Therapists — psychologists, marriage and family therapists, and clinical social workers — provide talk therapy. Many people do best with both, and Dr. Awerbuch is glad to coordinate with your therapist.

Do I need a referral?

Many patients contact the office directly. Some insurance plans require a referral from your primary care physician, so check with your plan or ask the office when you call.

Who does Dr. Awerbuch see?

Dr. Awerbuch’s outpatient practice focuses on adult psychiatry, including depression, treatment-resistant depression, anxiety disorders, OCD, bipolar and other mood disorders, addiction, and serious mental illness. If you’re unsure whether he’s the right fit, call the office — they can help you figure out next steps.

How long is the first appointment?

Your initial evaluation is longer than a typical follow-up so there’s time to understand your history, symptoms, and goals. The office will let you know how long to plan for when you schedule.

Will I have to take medication?

Not necessarily. Your treatment plan is a shared decision. Depending on your situation, recommendations may include medication, therapy, lifestyle changes, TMS, or a combination — and you’ll always have a say in what feels right.

Is TMS covered by insurance?

Many insurance plans, including Medicare, cover TMS for major depression when certain criteria are met — often after a number of medication trials. Requirements differ from plan to plan, so call the office to discuss your coverage.

Is what I share confidential?

Yes. Your health information is protected by federal and California privacy laws, including HIPAA. There are limited exceptions — for example, when someone’s safety is at immediate risk — which Dr. Awerbuch will explain at your first visit.

What should I do if I’m in crisis?

Please don’t wait for an appointment. Call or text 988 (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline), call 911, or go to the nearest emergency room. In Santa Barbara County, the Behavioral Wellness 24/7 Crisis & Access Line is (888) 868-1649.

Have a question that isn’t answered here?

The office is happy to help. Call during business hours or send a request, and someone will get back to you.
