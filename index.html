What happens when you reach out

Starting psychiatric care can feel like a big step. Here is how it works, from the first call to your plan.

  1. 01

    Reach out

    Call or email the office. Family members and referring clinicians are welcome to reach out as well.

  2. 02

    A brief conversation

  3. 03

    Your first appointment is an extended, in-depth psychiatric evaluation, so there is time to understand your full story.

  4. 04

    Your plan

    You leave with a personalized plan. Follow-up visits track your progress and adjust treatment as needed.

New to psychiatry

Questions people ask before a first visit

What’s the difference between a psychiatrist and a therapist?
A psychiatrist is a medical doctor who specializes in mental health. Psychiatrists diagnose conditions, consider medical factors that can affect mood and thinking, and prescribe and manage medication. Therapists provide talk therapy. Many people do best with both, and Dr. Awerbuch is glad to coordinate with your therapist.
Do I need a referral?
Many patients contact the office directly. Some insurance plans require a referral from your primary care physician, so check with your plan or ask the office when you call.
Will I have to take medication?
Not necessarily. Your treatment plan is a shared decision. Depending on your situation, recommendations may include medication, therapy, lifestyle changes, or a combination, and you’ll always have a say in what feels right.
Is what I share confidential?
Yes. Your health information is protected by federal and California privacy laws, including HIPAA. There are limited exceptions, for example when someone’s safety is at immediate risk, which Dr. Awerbuch will explain at your first visit.

Meet your psychiatrist

Adam W. Awerbuch, MD, MBA

Dr. Awerbuch earned a dual MD/MBA at the University of Miami Miller School of Medicine and completed his psychiatry residency at the University of Miami and Jackson Memorial Hospital.

During training he cared for patients with serious mental illness, substance use disorders, and HIV, and researched ways to better identify and support survivors of human trafficking. Today he sees patients in his private practice on Hitchcock Way, also practices at TheraMind Center of Santa Barbara, and serves on the medical staff of the Department of Psychiatry at Santa Barbara Cottage Hospital.
